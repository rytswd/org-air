#+title: org-air R98 — TAB pays for what it shows, and "dropped" is a family
#+air_state: complete
#+air_tags: org-air impl performance render project-view state-vocabulary

* Context

Two reports from daily use, neither of them from the test suite:

1. "expanding the hidden items using TAB feels quite slow; make it faster,
   or at least give some async hint that something is running and how the
   UI is locked up during that."  The user runs a *wide frame with the
   inline rail* — the column-composed =two-pane= orientation.
2. The project view's dropped fold "only knows one keyword."  Air's
   terminal-negative vocabulary is now a *family*: =off=, =out=,
   =dropped=, =canceled=, =cancelled=, and possibly more.

The supervisor's starting hypotheses were carried in as claims to *verify
or refute*, not as findings.  Both were measured first.

* Item 1 — TAB

** What was measured, and how

Corpus: =org-air-r95--scale-items= (the suite's own scale fixture) at 1000
and 3000 items over 8 files, all *Untracked* — one section holding every
item, which is the shape the report describes.  Board at 120x40,
inspector off, frozen clock, =emacs -Q --batch=, byte-compiled sources,
best-of-3 after a discarded cold sample.  Every measurement ran under an
explicit =timeout=; nothing was left unbounded.

*Deliberately NOT measured: 10k.*  The round's measurement budget capped
the corpus at 3000 items and 20 files, and a 10k run is exactly the
unbounded command that cost the previous seat 23 hours.  The 1k→3k pair
already separates the two hypotheses (one scales linearly, the fixed one
does not scale at all), so 10k would have bought a third point on a line
whose slope is already known.  Recorded honestly as *not measured*.

*** What that says — and what it refutes

- *Confirmed*: the supervisor was right that two-pane fell through to a
  full =org-air-view--render=; 67% of the toggle is under it, and 24% of
  the whole toggle is the *calendar month* being recomputed because the
  rail is recomposed by a render nobody needed.
- *Confirmed*: collapsed rendering is cheap.  A collapsed full render is
  0.039s at 3000 items; the same corpus costs 0.555s the moment TAB
  expands the section.  The cost is expansion, not scanning (R53 holds:
  neither path re-queries).
- *The dominant cost is per-row painting, not per-row semantics.*  Over
  half the self time is =string-width= + =truncate-string-to-width= +
  =string-trim-right= + GC — i.e. measuring and padding thousands of
  strings to build rows that a 40-line window will never show.  Faces,
  badges and sorting are *not* the hot spot.
- =expand-file-name= + =file-name-nondirectory= (≈6.4%) is the origin
  breadcrumb, rebuilt per item per repaint even with no filter active.
- *Refuted for this corpus*: =org-timestamp-to-time= does not appear in
  the profile at all.  It cannot — the scale fixture is undated.  The
  timestamp work is real on a dated corpus (measured separately below),
  but it was never the reported slowness.

** The fixes, in the brief's order of preference

*** (a-1) THE BOUND — the one that actually fixed it

=org-air-section-expand-max= (default 200).  An expanded section paints at
most that many rows; the surplus stays behind the *same* =…and N more=
row the board already had.  TAB on that row adds another batch
(=org-air-view--section-reveal=, a per-buffer alist of accumulated
budgets), so the section *pages* open.  Collapsing forgets the budget.

The budget is a render input, not a side effect: it is threaded through
the pane temp buffer, the bookmark/desktop state plist and the repaint
stamp, exactly like =org-air-view--expanded-sections=.  Pure UI state over
already-cached items — changing it never re-queries and never opens a
file (R53 intact).

*** (a-2) EVERY ORIENTATION SPLICES NOW

=org-air-view--render-section= used to build only the board-only body and
two-pane fell through to a full render with a comment admitting
"correctness first".  Both paths now call ONE composer,
=org-air-view--compose-body=, which dispatches on the live orientation, so
the spliced band is byte-identical to the full render's band *by
construction* rather than by two code paths agreeing.  Three things had to
be handled rather than wished away:

- the two-pane fill row carries the pane divider, so it is taken from the
  composer, not from the stored one;
- the band's trailing newline is restored to whatever it was (a footer
  follows it in two-pane, nothing follows it in board-only);
- two-pane keeps the inspector *inside* the rail, i.e. inside the band
  just rewritten, so its markers are re-bracketed at the tail — with point
  on the first item, the same order =--render= uses, or the R18
  equivalence law would break the moment two-pane started splicing.

The splice path also binds the compute-once dynamics
(=--render-partition=, =--render-displayed=, the classify cache) that only
the full render used to bind.  Without them the "fast" path re-derived the
visible set once per consumer.

*** (a-3) TWO O(n·log n)→O(n) REPAIRS ON THE SHARED RENDER PATH

- =org-air-view--sort-by-date= was the last date sort computing its key
  *inside* the comparator.  Now decorate/sort/undecorate.  Measured on
  3000 dated items in the Overdue bucket, memo cold on both sides:
  0.215s → 0.063s (1000 items: 0.073s → 0.012s), order byte-identical.
- =org-air-view--timestamp-time= is memoised on the timestamp *object*
  through a hash table that is =:weakness 'key=.  The key is immutable and
  scan-owned, so a hit is exact, and the entry cannot outlive the item —
  no bound, no invalidation hook, no staleness class.
- =org-air-view--passes-filter-p= short-circuits when no filter is
  active, so the origin breadcrumb is not composed to hand a string to a
  matcher that already said yes.  Same answer =--tokens-pass-filter-p=
  gives for the empty token list; a short circuit, not a second rule.

*** (b) THE FEEDBACK AFFORDANCE — why no new chrome

The brief allowed reusing =--render-loading= / the "scanning N/M" banner
if a large expansion could not be made fast.  It could: TAB is now
*constant time in section size* (0.025s at 1000 items, 0.028s at 3000).
Adding a progress banner to a 28ms operation would be chrome for its own
sake, and the banner belongs to *scanning*, which TAB deliberately never
does.

The honest affordance is the fold row itself, which the board already
owned.  Under an already-expanded but still-capped section it says

: …and 1803 more — press TAB here to reveal 200 more

instead of the collapsed section's byte-frozen

: …and 1803 more — press TAB on the title to expand

because TAB on the *title* would collapse the section, so the row has to
name itself as the target and say what the next press costs.  No timers
were added to the synchronous path.

** Measured before/after

=make=-identical environment, compiled, best-of-3, seconds:

| corpus | orientation | R97 render | R98 render | R97 TAB | R98 TAB | speed-up |
|--------+-------------+------------+------------+---------+---------+----------|
|   1000 | board-only  |      0.013 |      0.005 |   0.226 |   0.025 |     9.0x |
|   1000 | two-pane    |      0.013 |      0.005 |   0.214 |   0.025 |     8.6x |
|   3000 | board-only  |      0.035 |      0.008 |   0.588 |   0.028 |    21.0x |
|   3000 | two-pane    |      0.039 |      0.008 |   0.555 |   0.028 |    19.8x |
|  10000 | both        | not measured (corpus cap; see above) | | | | |

TAB no longer grows with the section: 1000→3000 items moves it 0.025s →
0.028s.  The collapsed full render is 2.6–4.9x faster too, from (a-3)
alone — that part helps every repaint, not just TAB.

** THE HONEST ATTRIBUTION — which fix bought what

Same build, =org-air-section-expand-max= forced to =nil= (bound OFF, so
only the splice + (a-3) are active):

| corpus | orientation | R97 TAB | R98 TAB, bound OFF | R98 TAB, bound ON |
|--------+-------------+---------+--------------------+-------------------|
|   1000 | board-only  |   0.226 |              0.123 |             0.025 |
|   1000 | two-pane    |   0.214 |              0.190 |             0.025 |
|   3000 | board-only  |   0.588 |              0.583 |             0.028 |
|   3000 | two-pane    |   0.555 |              0.511 |             0.028 |

So: *the bound is what fixed the report.*  The two-pane splice and the
O(n) repairs are worth 1.1–1.8x, which at 3000 items is 0.51s of frozen
frame instead of 0.56s — an improvement nobody would feel.  This matches
the profile exactly: if half the time is measuring and padding strings for
rows, the only way to win is to not build the rows.  The splice is kept
because it is what makes each *batch* cost a batch (the header, footer and
every earlier section stay byte-untouched) and because it removes the
24%-of-a-toggle calendar recompute; it is not claimed as the fix.

** Laws kept

- *R53, no scan on toggle.* Neither branch queries.  The old =more= branch
  actually called =(or org-air-view--items (org-air-query-items))=; the new
  one splices from =org-air-view--items= only.
- *R91/R92 scroll and landing.* Both toggle branches still move point
  after rendering, inside =--with-scroll-stable=; the =more= branch's
  landing target is now derived from =--displayed-items-for-bucket=
  (the rows the section actually shows) rather than from
  =--collapsed-window=, so a partly-paged section cannot land point on a
  row that was visible all along.
- *R18 equivalence.* One composer for both paths.
- No new timers.

* Item 2 — the dropped FAMILY

** The predicate and the list

=org-air-project-dropped-states= (defcustom, =:type '(repeat string)=)
defaults to

: ("dropped" "canceled" "cancelled" "out" "off")

and =org-air-project--dropped-state-p= is the ONE test that reads it
(case-insensitive; nil/non-string is not dropped-like).
=org-air-project--dropped-state-position= exposes the index, which the
sort rank uses so two dead states never tie.

*Why these five.*  =dropped= is airctl's own word.  =canceled= /
=cancelled= are one state spelled two ways and both are in real use.
=out= (handed off) and =off= (parked) were promoted in R80 to first-class
*live-ish* states; the user's report is that in practice they are just as
finished as =dropped= — nothing further will happen to them — and being
excluded from the fold made them clutter.

*Why =complete= is NOT a member.*  It is the *positive* terminal state,
the outcome the whole board exists to produce.  Folding it away would hide
finished work as though it had been abandoned, and the count that says how
much got done is the one number a planning view must never bury.

** Every call site now routed through the predicate

The nine hard-coded =(equal state "dropped")= tests named in the brief,
and what each became:

| what                                  | before                        | after                                     |
|---------------------------------------+-------------------------------+-------------------------------------------|
| row title face (=--doc-row-face=)     | =(equal state "dropped")=     | =--dropped-state-p=                       |
| fold partition (=--partition-dropped=), both arms | 2x =(equal … "dropped")= | =--dropped-state-p=            |
| fold group key (=--dropped-key-for=)  | =(cons 'state "dropped")=     | =(cons 'state DOC's own state)=           |
| section fold key (=--insert-section=) | =(cons 'state "dropped")=     | =(cons 'state (plist-get section :state))= |
| revealed-row landing (=--goto-dropped-row=) | =(equal … "dropped")=   | =--dropped-state-p=                       |
| TAB dispatch (=org-air-project-toggle=) | =(equal … "dropped")=       | =--dropped-state-p=                       |
| within-group rank (=--state-sort-rank=) | =(equal state "dropped")=   | three tiers, below                        |
| state face (=--state-face=) fallback  | =_ → faded=                   | family → =air-state-dropped=, else faded  |
| dir count rollup (=--dir-count-summary=) | display order only         | + the family, appended after it            |

=org-air-project--sections-by-state= now carries the bucket's raw =:state=
in the section plist.  That is what makes each family member fold
*independently*: without it, TAB on *Out* would have collapsed *Dropped*.

** Sort order

=org-air-project--state-sort-order= is now
=("ready" "work-in-progress" "complete" "draft" "out" "off")= and
=--state-sort-rank= reads three tiers:

1. a state with a *registered lifecycle slot* takes its position (0–5);
2. *unknown* — anything org-air does not recognise at all — ranks 6, past
   every live state, because an unrecognised state is a metadata bug on a
   doc that is probably still alive and must stay visible;
3. the *family without a registered slot* (=dropped=, =canceled=,
   =cancelled=, user additions) takes the terminal block from 7 upward, in
   list order.  Dead sorts after broken.

R80 had =out=/=off= at ranks 3/4, *above* =draft=.  R98 moves them to the
end of the live block.  This is not cosmetic: with the fold on, a family
member ranking above a live =draft= made the fold visibly incoherent —
collapsed, the =… N dropped= row sat at the group bottom; expanded, the
very same rows reappeared mid-list above the drafts.  Every pinned R80
relation survives (=out= and =off= still rank after =complete=, before
unknown, before =dropped=).  Verified ranks:

: ready 0  work-in-progress 1  complete 2  draft 3  out 4  off 5
: <unknown> 6  dropped 7  canceled 8  cancelled 9

** Badges — the ruling

*The family is about GROUPING, never identity.*  Nothing about the
approved badge vocabulary changed:

| state     | token   | letter | badge face                     | row title band |
|-----------+---------+--------+--------------------------------+----------------|
| out       | =OUT  = | =O=    | =org-air-face-air-state-out=   | dimmed         |
| off       | =OFF  = | =F=    | =org-air-face-air-state-off=   | dimmed         |
| dropped   | =DROP = | =X=    | =org-air-face-air-state-dropped= | dimmed       |
| canceled  | =CANCE= | =K=    | =org-air-face-air-state-dropped= | dimmed       |
| cancelled | =CANCE= | =K=    | =org-air-face-air-state-dropped= | dimmed       |
| complete  | =COMP = | =C=    | =org-air-face-air-state-complete= | NOT dimmed  |

- =OUT=/=OFF=/=DROP= and their colours are byte-unchanged; the family
  dims the *title band* (=--doc-row-face=), never the badge cell, which is
  how a receded row still reads =OUT= in OUT's colour.
- The two =cancel= spellings share =K=, not the bare initial: =C= is
  already Complete in the per-directory rollup, and a finished doc and an
  abandoned one must never print the same letter.  They share =K= with each
  other deliberately — one state, two spellings, one honest letter.
- A family state with no explicit face entry resolves to
  =air-state-dropped= rather than the generic faded face, so =canceled=
  reads as terminal-negative and not as an unknown-state metadata bug.
  A state *outside* the family is still faded; that distinction is the
  point.

** Unknown / future states degrade honestly

An unrecognised state (=experimental=, a typo, a state Air adds next year)
is *never folded and never hidden*:

- =--sections-by-state= appends any unlisted state as its own section;
- its token is the upcased 5-column truncation (=EXPER=), its face the
  generic faded one, its row title band *not* dimmed;
- it ranks 6 — after every live state, before the dead ones.

The one place an unknown state is still absent is the per-*directory*
count summary, which iterates a fixed letter order (display order, plus —
new in R98 — the family appended after it, so a =canceled= doc is counted
where its siblings are instead of disappearing behind the fold).
Example: =C2 O3 K1 K(+4)=.  Extending that loop to unknown states was
tried and *reverted*: it changes the pinned R22-6 rollup goldens
(=org-air-f5-project-view-byte-mockups=,
=org-air-r22-6-count-summaries-left-anchored=), which is a re-bless this
round was not authorised to make, and the honesty requirement is already
met more visibly by the section + row.  Recorded as a follow-up.

** The sibling axis that was deliberately NOT merged

=org-air-review.el= / =org-air-revisit.el= also speak of "dropped", but
that is a *different axis*: an Org TODO *keyword* (=CANCELLED=, =KILL=, …)
on a task heading, not an Air doc *state* on a planning doc.  It already
has its own family list and its own single predicate
(=org-air-view--dropped-keyword-names= / =--dropped-keyword-p=, R79/R84).
The two vocabularies are kept separate on purpose: a user's Org keyword
set has nothing to do with Air's document lifecycle, and collapsing them
would make customising one silently change the other.

** The three unexpected results, classified

Tests were NOT edited.

*** 1. =org-air-r95-19-repaint-is-linear-in-both-gate-modes= — OBSOLETE

: (should fold-expanded) :form fold-expanded :value nil

This is *leg 2*, an anti-vacuity probe, and it defines "the fold row
really expanded" as "no =…and N more= row remains".  R98 deliberately
replaces that invariant: expanding a 1200-member section paints one
200-row batch and *keeps* a fold row, now counting the 1000 still behind
it.  The fence's actual law — leg 1, ZERO item-wise linear scans over a
full repaint — still holds, as do legs 3, 4 and 5.

Proof that the bound is the only quarrel: the identical test passes
unmodified with =org-air-section-expand-max= set to =nil=
(=Ran 1 tests, 1 results as expected, 0 unexpected=).

*Re-bless shape* (one of, for the next seat that may edit tests):

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
;; minimal — the fence is about linearity, not about paging:
(let* ((org-air-section-expand-max nil)   ; add to the existing let*
       (files …))
  …)

;; or, keeping the bound and re-stating leg 2 as what it means now:
(setq rows (org-air-r95--rows 'untracked)
      fold-expanded (> (length rows)
                       (org-air-view--section-limit 'untracked)))
#+end_src

The second shape is the better pin: it asserts the probe revealed rows the
collapsed window had hidden, which is what leg 2 was always for, and it
stays true under any =org-air-section-expand-max=.

*** 2–3. =org-air-r26-3-legend-on-screen-popped= and =org-air-r49-4-project-inline-legend-first-windowful= — PRE-EXISTING, NOT R98

: (should (<= (1+ …) h)) :form (<= 27 26)
: (should (<= actions-line height)) :form (<= 31 30)

Both fail *identically on the unmodified parent* (=vzxoxyns / 92215f8f=,
R97): full suite, no =.elc=, sources restored byte-for-byte →
=Ran 1353 tests, 1343 results as expected, 2 unexpected, 8 skipped=, and
those two are the pair.  Bisected further: they also fail with only
=org-air-project.el= reverted, and with only =org-air-view.el= reverted.
R98 touches neither the rail's line count nor the Actions block.

Both assert that a *pathological, structurally-probed minimum* side-window
height (body 26 / 30) still keeps the Actions legend within the fold —
an off-by-one against =window-body-height= in batch.  They are
environment-sensitive by construction (the R26-3 docstring says so: "the
pathological minimum heights bump +2 accordingly, structurally probed"),
and this seat's Emacs/batch frame lands one line the other side of that
probe.  Left alone: re-probing them is a test edit, and this round was
told not to make one.
